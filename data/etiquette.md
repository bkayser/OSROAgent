---
title: Referee Etiquette and Best Practices
---

Navigating the transition from "certified" to "working ref" involves more than just knowing the Laws of the Game. In Oregon, where we rely heavily on local club volunteers and a centralized assigning ecosystem, your reputation is built on your **reliability** and **communication**.

Here is a guide to the professional etiquette expected within the Oregon Youth Soccer Association (OYSA) and local sanctioned tournaments.

---

# Requesting and Accepting Assignments

In Oregon, most assignments flow through **RefTown**. Your behavior on this platform is your "digital handshake" with the assignor.

### Games assigned to you

* **Set Availability Early:** Don't wait for assignments to be posted. Proactively block out your "Conflicts" in RefTown. Assignors hate "fishing" for referees only to have their offers declined.
* **The "24-Hour Rule":** When you receive an assignment offer, accept or decline it within 24 hours. Leaving an assignment in "Pending" status blocks the assignor from finding a replacement.
* **Assignment Priority:** Follow the USSF Priority Protocol. Generally:
1. International/National assignments.
2. Professional/College assignments.
3. USYS Regional/State League (e.g., OYSA Thorns/Timbers Leagues).
4. Local Recreational/Tournament games.

* *Note:* If you are offered a higher-priority game after accepting a local one, contact your original assignor immediately. Most Oregon assignors are supportive of your growth but appreciate as much lead time as possible.

### Requesting and claiming assignments

Many leagues will assign games to referees based on availability, but in many cases they will open up games for referees to request or claim, such as when an assignment was turned down.  Some leagues only offer games for self assignment and do not use availability.

In an ideal situation, an assignor will have for each field a crew of officials working as a team across a block of games, rotating their positions each game.  The refs will ideally have 3 or more games in a row.  This is good for fostering teamwork and managing the work rate of the individuals, as well as overall referee development.

It can be difficult for assignors to find referees willing to take one game by itself without games available before or after.

With that in mind follow these guidelines:

* Be mindful of your ability to keep focus and maintain energy.  Don't take more games than you can handle.
* Request adjacent games.  If looking for a block of games, avoid jumping from field to field between games if you can get a block on the same field, even if it means requesting a game or position that is not your favorite.
* Mix up the assignments: Don't request a block of 4 Center positions.  Alternate Center assignments with AR assignments.
* Don't leave "orphan" games at the beginning or end of the day when requesting a block.  For instance, if there are four games in a row, don't request the two middle games.  If requesting just one game, prefer the first or last game of the day so other referees can still fill blocks.

# Communication with the Assignor

Treat your assignor like a high-value client. They manage hundreds of referees; being the "low-maintenance" one earns their trust and respect and can help get more and better assignments.

When e-mailing your assignor with questions or special requests, be polite and professional.  Include all details when referencing a game: Date, time, field, position, and Reftown game number if handy.

## Turnbacks

"Turnback" is the term used to cancel an assignment you have already accepted.  If you are unable to work a game that you confirmed in Reftown, there may or may not be an option to turn back the assignment after you accepted it, depending on the organization's policy.

If you must return a game (illness, emergency), do it **immediately**. Avoid "Turnback Etiquette" sins like turning back a game on Friday night for a Saturday morning kickoff without a phone call.

* **In Reftown:** Use the turnback button and provide a short explanation.
* **By Phone:** If the turnback button isn't available it's because you're too close to gameday to cancel online.  You MUST phone or text the assignor and keep trying until you reach them.

# Communication with the Crew

A unified crew prevents 90% of sideline dissent. If the crew looks like a team, the coaches treat you like one.

* **The "Pre-Game" Briefing:** The Center Referee (CR) should lead a 5-10 minute discussion. Even if you've worked with the crew before, review:
* **Diagonal System of Control:** Confirm the diagonal and how the ARs will help with fouls in their "quadrant."
* **Bench Management:** Who is watching the coaches? (Usually the AR on the bench side).
* **Penalty Area Incidents:** How to communicate and signal fouls in and around the penalty area.

* **Half-Time Professionalism:** Stay together as a crew in the center of the field or a neutral area. Do not go to your cars or talk to parents. This is the time to adjust your "line" for the second half.

# Dress professionally:

* Wear official socks, shorts and shirts
* Wear black or mostly black shoes
* Tuck in your shirt
* No hoodies
* Socks pulled up
* No necklaces or earrings
* No hats, unless you need it to avoid sun exposure or to keep warm.  In those cases the hats should be black without logos.
* No warmups or tracksuit pants.  If you have multiple games and need to stay warm, wear black compression leggings or thermal layers underneath your socks and shorts.
* Nothing around the neck: Whistles are never put around the neck, stopwatches should not be avoided and should never go around the neck

Use proper gear:
* Use a wristwatch.  Don't use a phone or stopwatch to time games
* Use a proper referee's pealess whistle
* If you don't have official USSF gear, use as close to possible, always black.  Wear black soccer shorts, not volleyball shorts or sweatpants.  Wear black socks, pulled up.

Maintain a professional appearance for the entire time you spend at the field, not just during game time.

---

# Game Day Procedures

* **The 30-Minute Arrival:** Arrive at the field at least 30 minutes before kickoff, fully dressed in your primary (Yellow) uniform.
* **Stay on Time:** Know ahead of time what the competition team check in requirements are and allow time.  Don't wait until the last minute to walk the field, gather game balls and add air to them when necessary, have the pre-game meeting, check your equipment, set your watch, etc.  There is a lot to do so make sure you leave time.  

---

# Interaction with Team Staff

* **The "Vibe" Check:** Greet coaches with a brief, professional nod and "Good morning, Coach." Avoid long social conversations with one coach, as it creates a "perception of bias" for the other team.
* **Technical Area:** Make sure the teams set up at least 10 feet from the centerline.  Keep the centerline area clear.  This will help avoid problems later if the game temperature rises.
